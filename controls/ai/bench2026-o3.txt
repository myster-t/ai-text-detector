Global warming, the long-term rise in Earth’s average surface temperature, is driven principally by the accumulation of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the atmosphere. According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations climbed from 280 parts per million (ppm) in 1750 to 417 ppm in 2023, while methane surpassed 1,900 parts per billion (ppb). This rapid escalation has coincided with an estimated 1.1 °C increase in global mean temperature relative to pre-industrial levels, triggering more intense heatwaves, erratic precipitation, and rising sea levels.

Human pollution underpins this trend. The International Energy Agency reports that energy-related CO₂ emissions reached a record 36.8 gigatonnes in 2022, with coal-fired power plants alone responsible for roughly 40 % of that figure. Transportation adds another 8 gigatonnes annually, mainly through petroleum combustion. Agriculture amplifies the problem: livestock digestion and manure management produce about 32 % of anthropogenic methane, while nitrogen-based fertilizers emit nitrous oxide, a gas nearly 300 times more potent than CO₂ over a century. Deforestation compounds these releases by stripping away vital carbon sinks; the Food and Agriculture Organization estimates a net loss of close to 10 million hectares of forest each year between 2015 and 2020.

Mitigation strategies hinge on reducing emissions at their sources and enhancing natural sequestration. Transitioning to renewable energy is a crucial step. In 2022, solar and wind supplied 12 % of global electricity—up from 5 % in 2015—cutting an estimated 2.7 gigatonnes of CO₂ that fossil generation would have produced. Scaling this trend demands expanded grid infrastructure and storage; studies by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory project that batteries and pumped hydro could handle up to 80 % renewable penetration in major economies by 2040. Electrifying transportation offers parallel gains: the International Council on Clean Transportation found that lifecycle emissions of battery-electric vehicles are 60–70 % lower than comparable gasoline cars in regions where renewables exceed 50 % of power generation.

Carbon pricing provides an economic lever. As of 2023, 73 national or subnational jurisdictions operate carbon markets or taxes, covering about 23 % of global emissions and raising over US$95 billion annually—funds frequently reinvested in green infrastructure. Finally, restoring ecosystems magnifies natural carbon uptake: the Trillion Trees initiative projects that reforesting 350 million hectares could sequester up to 30 gigatonnes of CO₂ by mid-century.

Collectively, these measures demonstrate that curbing greenhouse gases is technologically feasible and economically viable. Their timely implementation remains essential if warming is to be limited to the Paris Agreement’s 1.5 °C threshold.
