This special essay explores the relationship between extreme intensities and extremality among several game-theoretic forms including 'flooding'. Extreme conditions and extreme intensities represent fundamental constraints imposed on the emergence, complexity, and behavior within such contexts, an aspect that has received little attention in the current literature. However, we argue above all of their importance as they offer a powerful framework into which theoretical debates can be brought back to reality while at the same time highlighting some important areas where theoretical considerations should not stop here and can actually serve to advance the discussion further. The first part focuses on the case of flooding, exploring certain basic characteristics relating it to different types of extreme environments. In particular, we focus on four factors underlying this phenomenon. First, extreme conditions in the real world are often limited by physical limits arising solely from those associated directly with our own bodies; e.g., floodwaters are typically bounded by oceans or other terrain features. Second, extreme situations require that actors have specific cognitive capabilities beyond those required under most realistic scenarios (e.g., understanding that flooding may occur and acting accordingly). Third, extreme situations provide distinct problems of scale – often leading people to ask what level of impact one's actions will cause given available resources. Finally, the extreme situation needs to allow sufficient scope for large external changes with consequences beyond the immediate interests of affected parties. These dimensions lead us not only to consider flooding within nonhuman context but also to take account of alternative perspectives – especially on external change theory. It thus stands out that even though we approach these studies through similar methodological lens, many conclusions can arise differently based on whether one begins with the perspective from inside or outside the body itself. Our main goal then remains threefold: to clarify how extremes emerge, how extreme conditions depend on players' cognitive powers, and how players navigate against the background of these effects that can affect all aspects of decision-making.
