Methods and limits

Binoculars (Hans et al., ICML 2024): the text's perplexity under a performer model divided by the cross-perplexity between an observer and the performer, which normalises for prompts and topics that make text predictable for reasons other than machine generation. This run uses a different model pair than the paper, so the published thresholds do not apply and none are shown; read the position relative to the controls instead. Input truncated to 512 tokens (258 scored here).

Fast-DetectGPT (Bao et al., ICLR 2024), analytic form: how much more probable the actual tokens are than tokens sampled from the observer's distribution, normalised by the expected spread. Machine text sits at a local maximum of probability (positive curvature); human text does not. Computed from the same two forward passes as Binoculars. Alignment imprint (after Wu et al. 2026, "LAPD"): mean per-token log-likelihood under the aligned performer minus under its base observer; text that follows the aligned model's preferences scores positive. This report uses the unweighted form.

Surprisal dynamics (Lastde, Xu et al. 2024; DivEye, Basani & Chen 2026; late-stage stability, Sun et al. 2026): statistics of the per-token log-probability sequence as a time series: spread, local volatility, lag-1 autocorrelation, and second-half versus first-half volatility. Shuffle shift (after La Cava & Tagarelli 2026): the log ratio of perplexity after shuffling the sentences to the original.

Token statistics (GLTR, Gehrmann et al. 2019; DetectLLM log-likelihood/log-rank ratio, Su et al. 2023): where each real token sat in the Qwen3.5-2B-Base model's ranking, and how spread the model's prediction was. Perplexity and burstiness: how predictable the text is and how much that varies sentence to sentence. Weak on short texts, formulaic genres and text dense with names or numbers.

Stylometry (lexical diversity: MATTR, Covington & McFall 2010; MTLD, McCarthy & Jarvis 2010; sentence and paragraph rhythm; punctuation and construction rates): model-free descriptions of register. They separate the controls only partly and shift with genre, so read them as context. Writing patterns and hidden characters: deterministic checks that need no model.

No detector proves authorship. Edited or paraphrased machine text can pass, formal human writing can be flagged, and every model-based score depends on the specific models used. Read these numbers alongside drafts, sources and the writer's history.
